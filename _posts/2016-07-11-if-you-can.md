- Traditional pension plans of our parent's age are disappearing, so need to invest well

**Hurdle 1 - Be frugal**

**Hurdle 2 - Learn about finances - read financial books/blogs**

- **Real Return** - Think in terms of Real return on your savings by taking into account inflation (about 2%)
  - ex: 5% return on savings - 2% inflation = 3% real return on investments
  - Should technically invest monthly adjusting for inflation also ($100, $102, etc) but income should be adjusting for inflation anyways so just invest a % instead of fixed amount
- **Stock** - sell ownership interest
  - No one can predict the market short-term but long-term it's steady
- **Bond** - borrowed money
  - W/ profit, you pay off lenders first and after excluding expenses of the business, you pay off residual owners of the stock
  - B/c lenders of bonds are risk averse they don't get to vote on company board (but stock holders do b/c they want to maximize profits)
  - --> thus corporate bonds generally bad deal, should confine to gov offerings
- **Investing assets**
  - high risk ones but potentially high returns - like stocks
  - low risk ones w low returns - T bills, CDs, money market funds
- Example
  - Treasury bonds - ~3.6% yield over 30 years w 1.4% yield after inflation-adjustment (-2%)
  - Domestic stocks - 2% yield + growth rate 1.5%
  - Thus 2/3 stocks, 1/3 bonds = ~3% return long-term
  - Spreadsheet of accumulated assets over time : www.efficientfrontier.com/files/savings-path.xls
- Consider vanguard group - you own shares vs other mutual companies

**Hurdle 3 - Learn about financial history (nothing is new, so understand where you fit in history of marketing/finance)**

- If economy is bad, future returns are high, vice versa
  - If friends recommend stock, or investment/real estate, may you shouldn't be whereas if they respond negatively to your investment strategy may it's a good sign
- Still can't Time the market despite this
  - use knowledge of financial history as emotional stabilizer to keep portfolio even keel and prevent you from going all-in to the market when everyone else is too or selling when economy is bad
  - Allows discipline of sticking to fixed stock/bond allocation and sell at high prices and buy at low ones

**Hurdle 4 - Maintain strict long-term discipline in saving and investing**

- Know yourself and fight against messing up your portfolio for short-term gains

**Hurdle 5 - Assume everyone is giving you bad advice (dont listen to the chatter), and choose your advisors very carefully**

- Avoid any stock broker, full-service brokerage firm, newsletter, adviser who purchases individual securities, hedge fund, most mutual fund companies, most financial advisors
  - stock brokers do not need to comply w fiduciary duty (vs advisor)- can be your friends, relatives so deflect any conversation
- Mutual fund industry lightly less hostile
  - due to 1940 Investment Company Act
  - but primarily focus on growing their funds (AUMs) (not your returns) although tighter linkage b/w two compared to brokerage
  - mutual fund or brokerage company has 2 owners = clients who buy mutual funds/stocks/bonds and shareholders who own stock of company itself
  - fund companies raise fund fees but you're unlikely to notice its effect for many years
  - except vanguard (has one owner)
  - you get charged more in *fees* than expense ratio which exceeded by transnational costs

**HOW TO**

- Steps
  - Max out employer match in 401k regardless of loans (return >50%)
  - Eliminate debt
    1. Credit card --> car loan --> student loans
  - Establish emergency fund
  - Establish emergency fund
    1. = 6 months living expenses in a taxable account
    2. T bills, CDs, money market accounts
  - Only then can you start saving for retirement (IRA, 401k, taxable accounts) = at least 15% of your income
    - Max out 401k match --> max out Roth IRA --> save in taxable account on top of that
    - In your contributions to 401k, IRA, taxable accounts - equally distribute b/w US stock, foreign stock, bond funds --> rebalance one a year
      - in good years sell some stocks - but avoid in taxable account since incur capital gains taxes
      - which means keeping stock holdings in a tax sheltered 401k or IRA
    - Index funds to use
      - vanguard funds, US total stock market index fund, Total international stock index fund, Short-term Bond index or Total bond market index fund, Or fidelity spartan series (total market index, international index, US bond index or short-term treasury bond index funds)
    - can slice/dice US and foreign stock options in smaller pieces (REITs, emerging/developed markets for foreign stocks)
    - more 401k are making "target funds"
      - ex: vanguard has target retirement funds - fees 0.16%-0.18%. 2060 fund had 90% stock which falls each %/year as person gets older
- Retirement Accounts
  - **Traditional IRA** - tax deduction on contributions, and pay taxes when money is wdrawn later (about a/f 60yo)
  - **Roth IRA** - contribute money you've already paid taxes on but pay no taxes on wdrawals in retirement
  - **401k** - similar to traditional IRA, can roll it into traditional IRA after you leave employer
    - Roth better deal than traditional b/c can contribute more to Roth (after tax worth more than pre-tax money) esp since you'll be in a higher tax bracket later
    - look at the fund offerings quality
    - if expense ratios >1% then it's a bad one --> only contribute up to the match
    - if expense ratio <0.5% and/or includes Vanguard's index funds or Fidelity's Spartan-class funds --> contribute in excess of match limits
- How to Spend down money in retirement
  - purchase inflation-adjusted fixed annuity (similar to traditional pension plan, or TIPS)

**Definitions**

- Expense Ratio
  - % of assets used for administrative, management, advertising etc.
  - Ex: 1%/year = 1% funds assets will be used to cover expenses each year
  - Doesn't include sales loads or brokerage commissions
  - small funds tend to have higher ratios
  - Typical US domestic stock fund ~1%, but passively managed funds (index funds) lower ratios
- Taxable Account
  - you pay taxes on investment income in the year it was received
  - Ex: individual and joint investment (savings) accounts, bank accounts, money market mutual funds (gov securities, T bills, CDs, commercial paper)
  - Make sure you declare investments that are taxable
  - Some investments are more tax-efficient than others
- Non-taxable Account
  - Retirement accounts (401k, IRA). Interests earned is not taxed.
  - But can be Tax-deferred
    - Taxed when you withdraw money (ex 401k vs Roth IRA)
- Mutual Fund Companies vs owners
  - investment made up of pools of funds (stocks, bonds, money market etc) operated by money managers
  - helpful for small investors - those who don't have enough money to diversify their portfolio. With other investors you pool money in to create wanted portfolio to create capital.
  - Shares = mutual fund units
  - Fund redeemed at fund's current Net Asset Value (NAV)
  - sometimes can get charged more in fees than expense ratio
