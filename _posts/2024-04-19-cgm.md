Recent few months I've been exploring fitness and health. The information out there is massive and you've really got to deep dive into the literature to simplify and find what ways work for you. Here I wanted to briefly write about the CGM experience.

CGM (continuous glucose monitoring)  
This was both a fun and stressful experience. I went deep into the glucose literature and have learned that in general glucose spikes, glucose fluctuations, and high glucose baselines are in generally bad. It causes inflammation with the spikes and glycogenating your cells just makes it age faster. And the less fluctuations/spikes and lower glucose baseline levels the better.

What I've learned is that:

- CGM is quite a useful tool as an accountability coach. When you become obsessed with seeing how your glucose changes after you eat something, you feel guilty eating carbs and sugars that tend to spike it more.
- I've learned that I could try to avoid sugar and complex carbs.
- Eating fiber (vegies etc) and vinegar will help attenuate those spikes
- Mixing fats can also do this, but then you have to watch your cholesterol / calories - there's always a give and take
- Eating only 80% full, or making sure you're hungry before you eat really helped control the glucose. I didn't realize I kept on eating when I really didn't need to. Still not sure if grazing is better or having timed meals would be.
- I was obsessing with veggies and protein for awhile, but then started to get protein spilling out of my urine and flank pain. I am now in the boat of eat what you use. Carbs is not bad if you use it especially if you are doing alot of cardio (zone 2). Protein is helpful if you're building muscles, but otherwise it can be hard on your kidneys if the excess if excreted in large amounts. There's some vague literature that might even show too much protein can be harmful as well but jury is still out depending on who you ask.
- I was meal prepping most of my food, and that took waaay too much of my time.
- Having the monitor on was uncomfortable since it does puncture your skin. Not that you feel it but the device itself does hinder my workout swim routines. Also, readings were inconsistent depending on if I put it on my arm vs abdomen. (used dexcom 7)
- My life revolved around controlling it that I don't think I enjoyed food at all, but it did help me be more conscious about what I'm eating.

I will continue to try the CGM occasionally to see if I've been able to improve my levels. There will soon be a noninvasive CGM in the next few years so will definitely be more likely to have that around. For now, moving on to the next longevity fad.
