I admit. I am one of those people who seldom follow through with my ideas. My hobbies are always in flux, and short-ended,  most of which would not even be pursued long-enough to be even considered a hobby – more like an interest of the month. Yeah, I can say it’s because I don’t have time, or energy, or because of a million other reasons. But really, I’m afraid. I’m afraid it won’t be good enough, it won’t be successful, it won’t be unique, and so on. In the end, I end up pursuing nothing.

I’ve worked hard to get this point where I can pursue a serious hobby. Now that I am here, it’s like trying to choose toothpaste amongst the overwhelming number of brands at a grocery store. I am paralyzed by freedom (or in this case capitalism).

It wasn’t until I started reading Elizabeth Gilbert’s book “Big Magic”  that I realized, first, its not an uncommon struggle, and second, there are other ways to think about pursuing your creative side.  She essentially reminded me that the creative process is happiness in itself. Who cares about whether it’s successful, or recognized, or any other value society puts to your pursuit. Go for it because it balances your life and brings value to it, and because you’re inspired by it and have your heart and soul in it.

Being a driven person, it was a nice reminder that sometimes you do things out of your intrinsic happiness even if it may not be up anything to society. I think I have put a lot of pressure on myself to pursue a useful hobby, to spend my time efficiently, and to make it perfect. But in the end, it only seems to have prevented me from enjoying my interest,  not to mention even starting one.

So, I’m going to just enjoy it from now on. And if my hobbies turn into something productive to the world, great. If not, that’s ok, too, because it made a difference in *my* life.
