I remember when I asked my uncle, who is a renown scientist in his field, how he is successful in research and publishing.

“You have to know just a little less,” he said. And it struck me where I was looking for answers.

After going through most of your career having seen the complexities, understanding the procedures it takes to get to a certain mastery level, it all becomes obvious and mundane. Yeah, If I want to do research on this topic, I know I have to do the literature review plan out the experiment, then execute it and write a paper for publication. Then repeat. Or, if I want to start a project or a business, I do my research, I execute and evaluate the outcome. The process still feels the same.

What I felt that I was missing in this process was what my uncle was able to highlight for me. If you think you know it all, there will be no questions to ask. By knowing just a little less about the world, he meant that you will preserve that innocent curiosity that will drive you do continue your work, even if you maybe going through the same physically and mentally steps.

That innocence, newness, or beginner’s mind is called Choshim (초심) in Korean (based on Chinese characters 初心 – composed of the characters ‘beginning/initial’ and ‘heart/mind’). In fact, it reminds me of an excerpt I read about Picasso’s work and how he later pursued child-like art that is untutored, untrained, unrestrained. He was quoted saying,

“Every child is an artist. The problem is how to remain an artist once we grow up.”

It is so easy to mindlessly go through the repetitive things I do in life. But I think it’s important to remind ourselves to retain Choshim, a goal I will try to achieve throughout the site.
